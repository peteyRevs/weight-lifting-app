## 1. Counted texts

**Fact.** `FeatureToday`'s catalog counts six keys (`%lld exercises`, `%@ of %lld sets`, `%@ of %lld sessions
this week`, `%lld weeks done. Start the next block from Program.`, `%lld min`, `~%lld min`), and all six were
looked up with the lifter's own locale. The new test, run on the host before the fix, recorded 93 issues. "N exercises"
alone was wrong under 20 of the 37 locales: `ja_JP` (with and without its calendar), `ko_KR`, `zh_Hans_CN`,
`zh_Hant_TW`, `my_MM`, `th_TH`, and the four with no known language ("", `und`, `zz`, `xx_YY`), where one is
"1 exercises"; `ru_RU`, `sr_Latn_RS`, `lt_LT` and `sl_SI`, where 21 or 101 take the singular; `fr_FR` and
`fr_CA`, where none is "0 exercise"; `fa_IR`, `hi_IN` and `hi_IN@numbers=deva`. The scan found the six lookups
outside the one place.

**Where the helper is, and why.** `LedgerKit/Sources/LedgerDomain/Units/CountedText.swift`:
`Locale.forCountedText(in catalog: Bundle) -> Locale`, public. `LedgerDomain` because it is Foundation alone
(standards §2), every feature already depends on it (`featureSpine`), and features do not import one another.
`LedgerDesign` is SwiftUI's and holds no lookup; `LedgerDependencies` holds dependency keys. **No dependency
edge was added** (`scripts/check-package-graph.sh` passes unchanged); `ProgramCopy.swift` and `WorkoutCopy.swift`
gained the line `import LedgerDomain`, a module their targets already depend on. The helper formats nothing
and names no text, so the Domain's own strings stay locale-independent.

**The catalog is each module's, so each module binds it once.** The helper must be told which catalog answers,
and `Bundle.module` is a different bundle in each module. Each of the three copy files (`ProgramCopy.swift`,
`WorkoutCopy.swift`, `TodayCopy.swift`) holds one member, `var forCountedText: Locale { forCountedText(in:
.module) }`, and every counted lookup is still spelled `locale: locale.forCountedText`. The two copies of the
logic (23 lines in `ProgramCopy.swift`, 27 private lines in `WorkoutCopy+Card.swift`) are deleted. Considered
and not taken: `locale.forCountedText(in: .module)` at each of the 18 call sites, which needs no member but
rewrites 11 lines of the Program's copy files and four uses in its tester's file for no gain.

**Fixed in `FeatureToday`:** `exercises`, `sets(done:of:)`, `sessionsSummary`, `blockCompleteBody`,
`estimate(minutes:)`, `duration(minutes:)`. No key, no English text and no catalog changed. What a lifter can
see: under a locale whose language counts otherwise than English, Today's counted texts now read as English
counts.

**Tests.** `FeatureTodayTests/TodayCountedTextTests` (new): each of the six texts over the Program tester's
37 locales and the counts 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 11, 21, 101, 999, with the count in the locale's own digits; the
region's grouping in seven locales; a scan that every key Today's catalog counts is looked up through
`locale.forCountedText` and no other lookup is (6 counted keys among 58 lookups).
`LedgerDomainTests/Units/CountedTextTests` (new, on the host too): the helper against catalogs made under the
temporary directory, of one language, of three (`fr_FR` takes French, `ru_RU` English), and of none.
**Measured there:** `Bundle.preferredLocalizations(from: [], …)` answers `["en"]`, so the helper's `if let`
never fails; the test pins that an empty catalog is taken as English.

**For Pete (standards wording, not edited here).** Standards §7 says "the locale picks more than the digits and
their grouping: it also picks the plural rule" and "the caller passes the injected `@Dependency(\.locale)`". The

## 2. Icons right to left

**Fact.** Every `LedgerIcon` is drawn by `LedgerIconShape`, a SwiftUI `Shape`, and a shape is mirrored in a
right-to-left layout. Measured before the fix (32-point icons, left to right against right to left, pixels that
differ by more than 8 levels): arrow 148, **check 204**, chevron 148, swap 140, minus 4, plus 0; each
right-to-left drawing was the exact mirror image of the other. `IconPillButton`'s other kind, an Apple symbol
by name, is not a path of ours: the symbol carries its own right-to-left form.

**Decision, by Apple's guidance** (Human Interface Guidelines, "Right to left": flip an icon that shows a
direction of reading or of movement; never a check mark):

| Icon | Used for | Right to left |
|---|---|---|
| `arrow` | "Start workout", "Resume" (forward) | mirrored, as before |
| `chevron` | a row's disclosure (forward) | mirrored, as before |
| `swap` | the swap-exercise button | mirrored, as before (see "For Pete") |
| `check` | the set row's check, the library's pick mark | **fixed: as drawn** |
| `minus`, `plus` | the steppers | as drawn (they are their own mirror images) |

**The fix, in one place.** `LedgerIcon.mirrorsRightToLeft` is the table; `LedgerIconView` draws the shape under
a left-to-right layout always and applies `.flipsForRightToLeftLayoutDirection(icon.mirrorsRightToLeft)`.
`ExercisePickerRow` (under `FeatureProgram/Views`) was not touched: its pick mark is `LedgerIconView(.check)`.
**Tried first and measured not to work:** `static var layoutDirectionBehavior: LayoutDirectionBehavior { .fixed
}` on the shape; the check mark's 204 pixels still moved (Mac host, Xcode 27.0).

**Test.** `LedgerDesignTests/LedgerIconDirectionTests` (new, host and simulators): each of the six icons drawn
left to right and right to left at `.body` and `.title` and at `.body` under `.accessibility3`. An icon of the
table is the pixel-exact mirror image where its square is a whole number of pixels, and leans as far to the
other side everywhere; every other icon is the same buffer (compared to a `Bool` first). Controls: the check
mark is not its own mirror image (more than 20 pixels), the signs are; a seventh icon fails a count until it
is given a direction.

**References.** Run at precision 1 over every phone reference of `LedgerDesignTests`, `FeatureProgramTests`,
`FeatureTodayTests`, `FeatureWorkoutTests` and `LedgerAppKitTests` (item 4's measurement), two differ after
the fix, both right-to-left, both in `FeatureProgramTests/__Snapshots__/ProgramSnapshotTests/`:

| Reference | What moved | Pixels |
|---|---|---|
| `rightToLeft_matchesReferences.library-dark-large.png` | the three pick marks, no longer mirrored | 272 of 391,950 (206 by more than 8 levels), rows 355 to 853 |
| `rightToLeft_matchesReferences.builder-dark-large.png` | the stepper's minus sign: it was drawn through the shape's mirror, whose rounding differs at its ends | 56 of 425,718 (16 by more than 8 levels), rows 133 to 142 |

Both old references still passed at precision 0.995 (allowed: 1,959 and 2,128 pixels). Both are recorded again
on the PR plan's iPhone 17 (iOS 27.0) so that they are exact. No left-to-right reference, no workout-cover
reference and no `PillButton` reference changed. #55 adds
`DayEditorSnapshotTests/rightToLeft_matchesReferences.dark-large.png`, recorded before this fix: if it shows a

**For Pete (a design choice).** The swap pair keeps turning round right to left, as it did. Apple's guidance
does not decide it: the pair shows an exchange, not a direction. Mirrored, its upper arrow points along the

test's table; no reference holds it right to left.

## 3. The hosted reader and text fields: not built

**Fact.** True, and already pinned on `main` as a limit:
`ProgramTextFieldTests.fields_areNotReadByTheHostedReader` (the header's tree is its heading alone; the search
field's is empty). A one-line `TextField` is a `UITextField` under the hosting view, and with the automation
switch on it is no accessibility element and has no element inside.

**Why nothing was built.** The brief said to stop if a `ProgramViews*Adversarial*` file needs the opt-in. One
does, and at a place no shared helper can cover: `ProgramViewsTreeAdversarialTests` reads `ProgramPage` (the
name field) at lines 41 and 45 and `ExercisePickerParts` (the search field) at 58 through `stops()`, and at
180 hands `ExercisePickerScreen` straight to `withAccessibilityElements(outsideScrollViews: true)`, where the
pinned search field stands outside the scroll view. The other callers go through
`Support/ProgramViewStops.swift` and `ProgramAccessibilityTests.swift`, which #55 edits. The orchestrator ruled
(2026-10-12): leave the reader as it is in this batch, and build it in one go after #55 merges.
`docs/testing-strategy.md` was not edited.

**Design.**

- A third parameter on the three readers in `LedgerSnapshotTesting/AccessibilityElements.swift`
  (`accessibilityElements`, `withAccessibilityElements`, `withLiveAccessibilityElements`), beside
  `outsideScrollViews`: `withoutTextFields: Bool = false`.
- In the walk (`elements(in:outsideScrollViews:)`), after the test for an element: a `UITextField` that is no
  accessibility element is left out whole (its subviews are not walked: while it is edited it holds a field
  editor, which is a scroll view), and unless the caller passed `withoutTextFields: true` the read fails with
  one `Issue.record`: the reader cannot read a text field in a package test process; read the field through
  UIKit (its placeholder, its text, its accessibility label, as `ProgramTextFieldTests` does), and say
  `withoutTextFields: true` where the tree is read without it.
- A field that *is* an element (if a later system or a view's own modifiers make it one) is returned like any
  other and fails nothing.
- A field inside a scroll view that the reader is already failing on is not reported a second time: the scroll
  view's failure says all of it is missing. (`ReaderCloseOutAdversarialTests` reads a `TextField` and a
  `SecureField` inside a scroll view and accepts only the scroll-view advice as a known issue; with this rule
  that tester's file needs no edit.) A field inside a scroll view that did yield elements (a `Form`'s rows) is
  reported.
- A multi-line field is a `UITextView`, a scroll view: the known limit F8-S2-8 stands as it is.
- Tests of the reader itself, both ways, in `LedgerDesignTests/AccessibilityReaderTests`: a field beside a
  text fails the default read and returns the text; with the opt-in the same read returns the text and fails
  nothing; a `SecureField` likewise; a view with no field reads the same with and without the opt-in; a field
  inside a failing scroll view gives the scroll view's failure alone; the three readers share the rule.

**Call sites that will need `withoutTextFields: true`** (or a `stops(_:withoutTextFields:)` parameter on the
Program helper that passes it on; the opt-in belongs at the caller, where a reviewer sees it, not inside the
helper):

| Where | What it hosts |
|---|---|
| `FeatureProgramTests/Support/ProgramViewStops.swift`: `stops`, `placedStops` | the parameter to pass on |
| `ProgramAccessibilityTests.swift` lines 32, 61, 63, 138 | `ProgramParts` of builder and library states |
| `ProgramViewsLayoutTests.swift` line 86 | `ProgramParts` |
| `ProgramTextFieldTests.swift` lines 118, 120 | `ProgramHeader`, `ExerciseSearchField`; the limit test becomes "fails by default, reads the rest with the opt-in" |
| `Adversarial/ProgramViewsTreeAdversarialTests.swift` lines 41, 45, 58, 180 | `ProgramPage`, `ExercisePickerParts`, `ExercisePickerScreen` (a tester's file) |
| #55: `DayEditorAccessibilityTests.swift` (8 reads), `DayEditorLayoutTests.swift`, `DayEditorTextFieldTests.swift` | `ProgramParts` of the day states, `DayEditorParts`, `DayEditorHeader`: the day's name, `NameField` |
| #55: `Adversarial/DayEditorViewsAdversarialSupport.swift` (2), `DayEditorViewsTreeAdversarialTests.swift` (3), `DayEditorViewsTitleAdversarialTests.swift`, `DayEditorViewsOrangeAdversarialTests.swift` | the same (testers' files) |

#55's notes say its name field is "the one accessibility element and the one `UITextField`". If the hosted
reader does return it as an element there, those reads need no opt-in: measure before editing them. #54 hosts no
text field (its diff holds none).

**Sentence proposed for `docs/testing-strategy.md` §2.5,** after "so a tree is never pinned from a reading that
silently lacks what scrolls":

> The reader cannot read a one-line text field either: in a package test process a `UITextField` is no

> a caller that pins the field that way reads the rest of the tree with `withoutTextFields: true`, which the
> reviewer confirms as for `outsideScrollViews: true`.

and in §2.6, after "The reader is loud": "A text field is the same: `withoutTextFields: true` is said where the
field is pinned through UIKit (`ProgramTextFieldTests`), never to pass a field over."

## 4. Reference precision, and a crop: investigated, nothing built

Measured with temporary probes in `FeatureProgramTests` and temporary edits to `LedgerSnapshotTesting`, all
removed; iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 27.0.

### (a) Precision 0.995 on a long page: true

`precision` is a fraction of the image, so what may differ grows with the page. A red square laid over the
middle of a page, drawn through the same strategy and compared with the page as the references are:

| Page | Image | May differ | Largest square that passes |
|---|---|--:|---|
| Builder, `.accessibility3` | 402 × 2,737 | 5,501 px | **74 pt** (76 fails) |
| Library, `.accessibility3` | 402 × 2,047 | 4,114 px | 60 pt (70 fails) |
| Builder, `.large` | 402 × 1,059 | 2,128 px | 44 pt (50 fails) |
| Library, `.large` | 402 × 975 | 1,959 px | 44 pt (50 fails) |

A real case from this batch: three mirrored pick marks (272 px) and a moved minus sign (56 px) passed their
old references (item 2). The longest reference on `main` is the design gallery's (402 × 8,176: 16,433 px may
differ, a 128 pt square).

**How much tolerance the references use today: none, on this machine.** With `SnapshotConfig.precision` set to
1 (perceptual precision unchanged at 0.98), all 248 phone snapshot assertions of the five targets were run:
every reference matched every pixel except the builder's right-to-left one (item 2's 16 pixels). The other
failures of that run were the five tests that pin the number 0.995. What the hosted runner needs is not known:
its references are the same files and pass at 0.995, and nothing has measured by how much.

### (b) A long page is drawn cut at its top and bottom: true, and wider than reported

Reproduced on `main`'s builder. The image keeps the measured height; the page drawn inside it is taller and is
centred, so it loses half the difference at each end:

| Builder | Measured (`sizeThatFits`) | Height of the page as drawn | First row of ink: in the reference, in truth | Cut at the top |
|---|--:|--:|---|--:|
| `.large` | 1,058.3 | 1,059 | 29, 29 | 0 |
| `.xLarge` | 1,267 | 1,272 | 28, 30 | 2 |
| `.accessibility1` | 1,937.7 | 1,960 | 20, 31 | 11 |
| `.accessibility3` | 2,737 | 2,752 | 26, 34 | 8 |
| `.accessibility5` | 4,469.3 | 4,486 | 29, 37 | 8 |

("In truth": the same view top-aligned in a window 200 pt taller, through the library's `.fixed` layout.)

**Cause.** The library measures the view before it applies the traits
(`hostingController.sizeThatFits(in: .zero)` in `SwiftUIView.swift`), on the simulator's screen, at 3×, where
SwiftUI rounds each view's size to a third of a point. It then draws at `displayScale = 1`
(`SnapshotConfig.scale`), where each is rounded to a whole point. Forty lines of `.body` text at
`.accessibility5` measure 40 × 52.33 = 2,093.3 pt and are drawn 40 × 53 = 2,120 pt high; forty lines of the
system's body font at `.large` measure 813.7 and are drawn 840. So it is not the text field, not `PillButton`
and not accessibility sizes as such: every stack of views whose heights are not whole points grows by up to
two thirds of a point a view. Confirmed from the other side: the same hosting controller measured inside a
window whose `displayScale` is 1 answers 2,753 for the builder at `.accessibility3`, not 2,737. A
`traitOverrides.displayScale` on a controller that is in no window changes nothing (measured: 0 of 248).

**How many references are cut.** Measured both ways for each of the 248 phone assertions on `main`: **99
differ**, by 1 to 23 pt (44 in `LedgerDesignTests`, 39 in `FeatureTodayTests`, 13 in `FeatureProgramTests`, 2 in
`LedgerAppKitTests`, 1 in `FeatureWorkoutTests`; 44 at `.accessibility3`, 27 at `.large`, 24 at `.xLarge`, 4 at
`.accessibility5`). The largest: the design gallery, dark, 8,176 against 8,199 (11 pt off each end);
`sixteenWeeks` at `.accessibility3`, 18; the builder at `.accessibility3`, 16; the type ramp, 14. #54's and
#55's new references are drawn the same way and were not counted.

What a cut reference still proves: everything but its first and last few points, and that those do not change.
What it hides: a change in the page's top or bottom padding, or in its last row, within the cut.

### Proposal (one harness change, then one `chore(snapshots):` pull request)

Neither fix meets this batch's bar (changes no reference, under about 30 lines): the first changes 99
references, the second changes a number that testers' files and the strategy pin.

1. **Measure at the scale the image is drawn at.** In `assertPhoneSnapshot` (`PhoneSnapshot.swift`): host
   `view.snapshotAppearance(appearance)` in a `SceneLessWindow` whose `traitOverrides.displayScale` is
   `SnapshotConfig.scale`, take `sizeThatFits(in: .zero)`, and hand the library
   `layout: .fixed(width:height:)` in place of `.sizeThatFits`. About 15 lines, in the one function every
   phone snapshot goes through. A test of the harness: forty lines of text whose height is not a whole point
   are drawn whole (first and last rows of ink as in a taller window). **Cost:** 99 references on `main`
   recorded again, each 1 to 23 pt taller, plus whichever of #54's and #55's are cut: well over the 20-PNG
   rule, so a PR of its own, after both merge. One `scripts/check.sh --record-snapshots` (about 10 minutes
   here), one full gate to prove them (about 12), and one hosted run. The watch path (`ImageRenderer`) is not
   affected.
2. **Give the tolerance in pixels, not as a fraction.** Once the harness knows the image's size (step 1), it
   can pass `precision: 1 − budget / (width × height)` with one `SnapshotConfig.pixelBudget`, so a long page
   is held as tightly as a short one. On this machine a budget of 0 passes today; the number has to come from
   the hosted runner. **Cost:** one hosted run on a throwaway branch with precision 1 to learn the runner's
   noise (its failed bundle lists each reference's matching fraction; about $1 to $2); then the five tests that
   pin 0.995 (`SnapshotMatrixTests` and the comparator's and the diff's adversarial suites: testers' files), the
   watch comparison's own use of `SnapshotConfig.precision` (`PixelComparison`), and the "Precision" sentence of
   testing-strategy §2.5, which is Pete's wording ("set once in `SnapshotConfig` and never overridden per
   test" still holds). No reference changes for it.

Step 1 first: it re-records everything once, and step 2 then tightens against exact references. Until then a
reviewer of a long page's reference should not read its first and last 10 pt as the layout's, and a small
visual change on a long page has to be shown by a test that measures it, not by its reference.

## 6. The font adversarial suites on the Mac host

**Fact.** True. `swift test --filter Adversarial` in `LedgerKit/` on `main`: four `LedgerDesignTests` suites
failed with 52 issues, and pass on both simulators: "Archivo loading (adversarial)" 8, "Bold Text
(adversarial, round 2)" 18, "LedgerFont rendering (adversarial)" 16, "Watch type floor (adversarial)" 10. Every
issue is a `#require` of the font read from the file (`ArchivoFile.url`, `.postScriptName`, `.swiftUIFont`).

**Cause.** The helper `ArchivoFile.url` looked for `LedgerKit_LedgerDesign.bundle/Archivo[wdth,wght].ttf`. iOS
and watchOS bundles are flat; the Mac host's build makes
`LedgerKit_LedgerDesign.bundle/Contents/Resources/Archivo[wdth,wght].ttf`. `LedgerDesign` itself finds the font
on the host (`Bundle.module.url(forResource:)`), which is why the implementer's font tests passed there.

**Which, and why.** They now **pass on the host**: the helper looks in both places (three lines). The other
choice, compiling the four suites for iOS only, would have edited four testers' files and taken 52 checks off
the host for a missing path. After the change the whole `--filter Adversarial` run is green on the host,
and the suites pass on the iPhone simulator as before.
